# Grand Games

A puzzle studio whose games are known for precise execution rather than new ideas. The lessons below are
about how it chooses, builds and runs games. Genre lessons are in the genre files listed above.

## How it chooses a game

**grand-games/1 — Follow proven download volume.** Pick a puzzle type that already tops the download charts
on ad revenue. Its demand is proven and its ads are cheap; the open question is only how to earn more per
player. *Check:* the game's concept names the proven rivals and their download scale.

**grand-games/2 — Speed of iteration is the edge.** *Estimated by the source:* the studio's version began to
scale about a year after the genre did, with levels and progression already tuned, while larger rivals had not
entered. *Our inference:* the first 50 tuned levels are a useful milestone. *Check:* the plan has a date for
the first playable, the first tuned levels and the first ad test.

**grand-games/3 — Many shots, a stacked portfolio.** The studio keeps several games live, each profitable and
stable, and adds new ones as genres trend. It does not need one big hit. Follow-up games for the next trend
are often built before they are scaled. *Check:* for a studio plan, each game has its own break-even target,
and the next concept is in work before the current one peaks.

## How it builds

**grand-games/4 — Add the fail state the genre lacks.** Its versions of ad-led genres add a way to lose (a
timer, lives) so purchases have a reason. See `arrow-escape/1`. *Check:* the concept names the fail state
that the genre's leading games lack.

**grand-games/5 — Sectioned levels are the signature.** Across its games, large levels open one section at a
time. It lowers the load on the player and adds beats inside a level. See `arrow-escape/4`. *Check:* the
level plan says from which level boards open in sections.

**grand-games/6 — Execution over invention.** The core action is the genre's. The work goes into the curve,
the fail state, the economy, the polish and the ads. *Check:* the concept says what is copied from the
genre and what is improved, and the improved part is where the effort goes.

## Money

**grand-games/7 — Earn from purchases, not only ads.** The studio adds purchases to genres that earn mostly
from ads, and stages its ads in late. *Estimated by the source:* about 30 to 40% purchase revenue for the
reviewed game, more than the studio's other games; the reviewers did not agree on the studio's usual share. See
`arrow-escape/11` and `arrow-escape/12`. *Check:* the economy table sets a purchase target as a share of
revenue, not only an ad target.

## Growth

**grand-games/8 — Wide ad production across several networks.** Many ad variants from a few concepts, across
at least two networks. See `arrow-escape/14` to `arrow-escape/16`. *Check:* the ad plan lists the concepts,
the variants of each and the networks.
